Cyber risk management is the disciplined process of identifying, analysing, treating, and monitoring the events that could stop the organisation from meeting its objectives through the loss or compromise of information systems. It is not compliance. It is not security operations. It is the layer above both that connects technical exposure to business decisions.

## The four questions cyber risk management must answer

1. **What can go wrong?** — Threat, vulnerability, impact.
2. **How likely is it and how bad?** — Qualitative triage, quantitative refinement for material risks.
3. **What are we going to do about it?** — Accept, mitigate, transfer, avoid.
4. **How will we know it's working?** — KRIs, control tests, assurance.

The rest of this course is a detailed answer to those four questions, module by module.

## What it is not

- It is not the same as **cyber security operations**. SecOps executes the controls; cyber risk management decides which controls are worth executing.
- It is not the same as **compliance**. Compliance proves you meet the rules; cyber risk management decides which rules matter for your business and where they leave gaps.
- It is not the same as **IT risk**. IT risk covers availability, project, and operational failures. Cyber risk is a specific subset focused on adversarial and information-security events.

## The line of sight from risk to control to evidence

A defensible cyber risk program can trace every control back to a risk it is designed to reduce, and forward to the evidence that proves it is operating. The [Unified Control Framework](../module-03-frameworks-and-baselines/README.md) is the artefact that carries the linkage. If you cannot draw that line, you are running a compliance calendar, not a risk program.
